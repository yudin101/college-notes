# Macroeconomic Policies

Set of government rules and regulations to control or stimulate the aggregate indicators of an economy frames the macroeconomic policy.

## Types of Macroeconomic Policies
- Fiscal Policy
- Monetary Policy

## Fiscal Policy
Macroeconomic policy where government makes changes in government spending or tax to stimulate growth.

### Types of Fiscal Policies
- Expansionary Fiscal Policy
- Contractionary Fiscal Policy

#### Expansionary Fiscal Policy
- Increases aggregate demand by increasing government expenditure or by lowering taxes
- Increases consumption as purchasing power increases
- Businesses get easy access to credit -> investment in new projects -> increase in GDP
- Adopted during economic depression

#### Contractionary Fiscal Policy
- Descreases aggregate demand by reducing government expenditure or by increasing taxes
- Decrease in consumption as purchasing power decreases
- Less capital is available to businesses -> economy contracts -> unemployment
- Adopted to control inflation

### Tools of Fiscal Policy

#### Budget
- The master financial plan containing a preliminary approval of estimates of public expenditure and revenues
- Presented to the Parliament by the Minister of Finance in the month of Ashadh, each fiscal year

##### Types of Budget
- **Balanced Budgetary Policy**: Government keeps its total expenditure equal to its revenue
- **Deficit Budgetary Policy**: Government spends more than its expected revenue
- **Surplus Budgetary Policy**: Government keeps expenditure substantially below its current revenue

#### Public expenditure
- The expenditures made by public authorities 
- Incurred in the form of expenditures on:
    - administration and maintenance of law and order
    - education, health, transport and communication, public works
    - providing social security (i.e. old-age pensions, unemployment allowances, etc)
    - providing economic and/or social overheads (transport and communication, health and education, electricty/drinking water, etc)

#### Public Revenue
- The income collected by the government through taxes, grants, fees, fines, borrowing, profits, special assessments, etc.

#### Public Debt
- Debt the government owes to or to the nationals of other countries
- Has become a normal method of government finance in the modern times

## Monetary Policy
Macroeconomic policy practiced by monetary authority (central bank) to achieve predetermined objectives through the changes in money supply, credit rate and interest rate in the economy.

### Types of Monetary Policies
- Expansionary or ease or cheaper monetary policy
- Contractionary or tight or dearer monetary policy

#### Expansionary/Ease/Cheaper monetary policy
- Increases money supply or monetary base or its rate of growth
- Can use its (central bank's) tools, individually or collectively to expand the supply of money 
- Can purchase treasury bills and bonds in open market, decrease the bank rate or discount rate and reduce the required reserve ratio to increase the money supply in the economy 

#### Contractionary/Tight/Dearer monetary policy
- Decreases money supply or monetary base or its rate of growth
- Can use its (central bank's) tools, individually or collectively to decrease the supply of money 
- Can sell treasury bills and bonds in open market, increase the bank rate or discount rate and increase the required reserve ratio to decrease the money supply in the economy 

### Tools of Monetary Policy

#### General/Quantitative/Indirect Instruments of Monetary Policy
##### Open Market Operation (OMO)
- When central bank buy/sell securities to the country's private banks
- When CB buys, it adds cash to the bank's reserves -> gives them more money to lend
- When CB sells, it reduces cash from the bank's reserves -> gives them less money to lend
- Buys when it wants expansionary monetary policy
- Sells when it wants tight monetary policy

##### Bank Rate or Discount Rate
- Interest rate at which the private banks can borrow funds from the central bank
- Bank rate high -> commercial bank borrowing from central bank is expensive -> commercial bank lending rate rises
- Cost of borrowing becomes high -> discourages investment -> (employment, output, income) decline
- Opposite happens when bank rate or per discount rate is lower

##### Minimum Reserve Requirement Ratio
- Portions of reserves that banks hold but don't loan out
- Compelled by law
- If decreased, more money is availble to commercial banks that expand credit/increase money supply
- But when commercial banks are holding excessive liquidity, the change in this ratio has no meaning

#### Selective/Qualitative/Direct Instruments of Monetary Policy
- Margin requirement while rediscounting or lending
- Regulation of customer credit
- **Rationing of credit**: Used to control credit provided by commercial banks (i.e. max loan limits/advances)
- **Moral Suasion**: Advising/Persuading commercial banks to implement central bank's monetary policy
- Use of publicity
- **Direct Action**: 
    - Direction issued by central bank to commercial banks regarding lending and investment policies
    - Used to enforce both quantitative and qualitative credit control
- Interest rate ceiling

## Key differences/similarities between expansion and contractionary
- Expansionary policies are used to tackle a fall in the overall production in the economy (GDP)
- Contractionary policies are used during inflationary periods, where this is growth in output along with growth in price
- These policies can be enacted to bring an economy back to full employment output
- The type of policy to be enacted depends on whether the output gap is positive or negative
- Both expansionary and contractionary monetary policies impact the aggregate demand, the price level, the real GDP, and the interst rate
